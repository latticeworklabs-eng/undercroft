# Work that compounds

Choosing work whose output feeds back into capacity — learning that accelerates learning, an audience that grows an audience, tools that build tools — so returns are exponential rather than linear. The defining hazard: exponential curves are indistinguishable from flat ones early, which is exactly when people quit.

## From [[2026-05-04-how-to-do-great-work]]

- [[paul-graham]] argued great work disproportionately comes from compounding processes; learning and audience are his two canonical examples.
- "The trouble with exponential growth is that the curve feels flat in the beginning." — the failure mode is abandoning the curve during the flat stretch, mistaking early-stage exponential for permanent linear.
- Practical implication: bias project selection toward things that leave reusable capital behind (skills, code, writing, audience), and judge them on trajectory, not this month's output.
- [[curiosity-as-compass]] is the complement: compounding says what *shape* of work to pick; curiosity is the fuel that survives the flat part.
