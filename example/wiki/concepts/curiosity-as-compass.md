# Curiosity as compass

The claim that sustained, specific curiosity is the most reliable selector of what to work on — beating prestige, planning, and top-down importance rankings — because what fascinates you is information about both fit (aptitude, plus appetite for the parts others find tedious) and opportunity (the gaps you can't stop noticing).

## From [[2026-05-04-how-to-do-great-work]]

- [[paul-graham]] ranked curiosity first among "the three most powerful motives" (curiosity, delight, the desire to do something impressive).
- Mechanism: interest buys hours and attention → you reach the frontier faster → frontier position is what makes gaps visible at all (steps 2–3 of his four-step recipe).
- Selection cannot be done by analysis from the outside: "The way to figure out what to work on is by working."
- Named failure mode: chasing prestige mimics ambition but routes you into crowded, well-lit problems where the compounding has already been captured.
- Feeds [[work-that-compounds]]: curiosity is what keeps you on an exponential curve through its flat early years.
